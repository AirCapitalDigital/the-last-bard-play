The Last Bard: Alpha 0.5.13.0 "The Hunt Evolves"

Andy's hunter weapons learn new tricks, and the Crucible gets creatures of its own.

New

  • Hunter weapons evolve. Each of Andy's five hunter weapons now evolves with weapons of other families: 22 evolutions in all. The Slayer's Horn, the hardest to earn, has ten of them and is the strongest of all: its hounds stampede, burn, chain lightning, poison, dash like a drawn blade, or run the strings of the Harp of Light, and every elite or boss they bring down adds one more hound to the pack for the rest of the run (up to six).
  • The other hunter weapons evolve too: a firebird whistle, a lightning bullroarer, snares that burn or sing, bone shards that sing or fly as blades, and more. Found as you play, and Maestra can teach them once you own the hunter weapon.
  • Hunter weapons fuse with weapons of their own family, like every other weapon.

The Crucible, since it opened

  • Its own creatures. Every foe inside is a new creature of the volcano, each with its own attacks, and every Movement ends with a new champion. Each Movement teaches something you will need at the top. Every creature comes in variations.
  • Walk before you climb. You arrive at the edge of the arena; a trail of notes leads you to a pedestal where you choose any Ember weapon you have found (and a boost, with the Booster Pack).
  • A reward chest after every wave, rising in the middle under a pillar of light: an ordinary chest, or a champion's hoard after a champion. Inside: one card, a piece of gear, or supplies, each on its own tablet. A Crescendo reward lets you choose between three.
  • Everything comes from the rewards: no XP orbs and no chests drop inside, and nothing that would do nothing there is offered (no XP, pickup range or drop luck).
  • You go in as yourself: your own look from the Mirror, without your gear or vanity. What you find inside is what you wear.
  • Health is locked between waves: regeneration stops when the last foe falls and resumes with the next wave. Supplies and power-ups still heal.
  • Lava burns, with a burning health bar and a countdown; the lava throws rocks and something in it spits fire (a pillar stops it).
  • Flawless counts only on a champion's wave, and the achievements for it now count champion waves and a flawless climb.
  • Fewer callouts: only the waves and the bosses are announced.

Changes

  • Your Crescendo plays on Ember Island as it does in the Sanctuary.
  • Andy's hunt panel is hidden inside the Crucible for now.
  • The Old Wolf's Eyepatch and Boots give 7% crit damage (was 8%).
  • Sharper floors on phones in the Crucible and on Ember Island.

Fixes

  • Crucible creatures no longer get stuck on the basalt pillars.
  • The Kiln Toad fires exactly along the line it shows, and its fire runs out along it.
  • Coming from Ember Island, the Crucible's creatures could appear as old Husks.
  • Entering the Crucible could leave you behind an empty screen on a phone.

The Last Bard: Alpha 0.5.12 "Ember Island" and "The Crucible Opens"

Jad sails in on his black boat and takes you to Ember Island, with eight new songs from the mountain.

The gate in the mountain is open: climb the Crucible, sixty-three waves to the Oldest Note.

New

  • Jad arrives. Once you have beaten the Drum Lord, the Choir and the Phantom Orchestra (each at least once), a hooded sailor named Jad moors his black boat at the Sanctuary's north pier. Talk to him to hear about the island and the trial inside its volcano.

  • Ember Island. Sail with Jad to a volcanic island and explore it on foot: step off his boat onto the jetty, cross the black beach, visit his camp by the fire, the forge, the shrine of the fallen and the Board of Records, and cross a great old bridge over a chasm of lava to the gate in the mountain. His boat takes you home whenever you like.

  • Eight new songs. Ember Island has its own music: intense 8-bit and synthwave tracks that play while you explore and shuffle when one ends.
  • Power-ups in the Journal. Every power-up, its picture, where it drops and what it does.
  • The Crucible is open. Walk up to the gate on Ember Island and enter the trial inside the volcano: 63 waves in seven Movements of nine, a champion at the end of each Movement, and at the very top, Tharrun, the Oldest Note. You go in with nothing but your Fountain upgrades, choose an Ember weapon, and build your climb wave by wave. One life, no revives.
  • The rules, before you go in. The gate opens a few short pages, each with a little animation: how the climb works, what comes in with you (only the Fountain), Ember weapons, the reward after every wave, the mountain's trials, and the prize at the top.

  • Ember weapons. Every weapon inside the Crucible is its Ember version, and any hit can set a foe alight.

  • A reward after every wave: choose new cards, a piece of gear for the climb, or supplies. Clear a wave without a hazard hitting you for double Echoes and a richer reward.
  • The mountain teaches. Lava, eruptions on the beat, meteors, scorched ground and the champions' great blows hit hard and ignore armour: keep moving. Pillars stop shots, Ember Conduits shield the champions until you break them, and a big attack you dodge leaves a champion open. Each trial is shown the wave it begins.
  • The prize. Reach the top and the Cape of Embers is yours, with the Ember skin and the Crescendo of Embers: a wall of fire that sets everything it touches alight, then pools of lava that erupt under the thickest crowds. The burn takes a share of each foe's full health, so it keeps up however tough they grow.

  • The Ember skin everywhere. Once earned (and switched on in Settings), your cards, weapon and gear icons, your weapon and boost tiles, the weapons you carry and every attack take on obsidian and fire.
  • 28 Crucible achievements, from your first climb to a flawless last wave, with 10 flaming titles for the leaderboards and the Mirror, and six pieces of ember vanity to wear.
  • Three Crucible pets: a salamander, a magma snail and a cinder crab, found by climbing far enough.
  • Your first landing on Ember Island is a short scene: the island's name rises, and the camera shows you Jad's camp, the Board of Records and the gate.
  • Back to the island. A climb ends back on Ember Island, and Jad has a word about how far you got.
  • The Wind Harp on Ember Island's beach works like the Magic Harp in the Sanctuary: pick any of the Crucible's songs and it plays at once. When it ends, the island picks the next one, as before. Tharrun's own song joins the list once you have beaten him.

Changes

  • Ember Island has its own pause menu and the Menu and mute buttons, as in the Sanctuary.
  • Ember Island works like the Sanctuary. The place in reach wears the same gold outline, name plaque and button, and you can tap or click anywhere on it to use it: Jad, his boat, the Board of Records, the forge, the shrine and the gate. Jad gets the outline on the Sanctuary's pier too.

  • Rowan and Morwen attack with more flair. Morwen raises her staff, the orb gathers light and she casts a bolt from it; Rowan pushes her bow out to aim, and it kicks back as the string snaps.

  • Every biome has its full name, in the corner of the screen, on Andy's hunts, in the Journal and on keepsakes: the Sunlit Meadow, the Whispering Wood, the Frostbound Tundra, the Silent Hollow and the Parched Reach. "Lands" are now "biomes" everywhere.

  • A badge for your card mode. Once you choose what the cards give after every real one is taken, the corner shows it: LOOT MODE (a gold chest) or ENDLESS (a blue card).
  • The "Find a village" task is gone. Its common gear piece now comes with "Visit 2 biomes in one run", so you still get the full common set.
  • Spek and Doug only go bonking the residents while they are walking with you and no menu is open, and you only hear them when they are on screen.
  • Most bosses counts every boss you fell. Each boss in a group and every boss that turns up late in a long run now counts on the leaderboard. Before, a run topped out at about four however many you beat.
  • Longest run is fairer. Past the half-hour mark, the clock only counts the time you truly survive.
  • The Crucible only offers weapons you have earned: the Ember versions of weapons you have found out in the world, and Andy's hunter weapons once you have bought them (they can now be your starting weapon there too). Fusions and evolutions can still come up whatever you have found, and a first one joins your Collection.
  • The Cape of Embers burns roots away before they can hold you.
  • Andy calls you by your rank, not always "whelp", and his hunt board shows all three hunts in a row with a clearer line about the Marks you can spend.
  • Tap or click anywhere on a highlighted place to use it, in the Sanctuary and on Ember Island, as well as its button.
  • The button over a place always stays on screen. For something tall, like the Crucible's gate, it now sits on the place itself instead of above the top of the screen.
  • Power-ups have their own page in the Journal, after the Bard's College, instead of the end of the Foes list.

  • The Crucible's entrance keeps its secrets: a shorter set of pages about surviving the waves, and the Echoes you earn inside are yours to keep.
  • The Board of Records only seems to keep count of something until you clear your first wave.
  • Molten synergy strings with the Ember skin, in the HUD and the pause menu.
  • One wave-end banner in the Crucible: the wave, its Echoes, and FLAWLESS on a clean champion's wave.
  • Crescendo choice cards show only what each one does.

The Last Bard: Alpha 0.5.11.0 "First Verses"

A guide for your first runs, gentler early minutes, two new pets, three save slots, and a Crescendo for every style.

New

  • First Verses. Small goals ("verses") that teach the game from your very first run: survive, collect XP, smash crates, open chests, max out a weapon, fuse and evolve, find the Resonant Stone and more. Five chapters, sung in any order, in any run.

- Every verse pays Echoes the moment it is done, with a callout and a line on the death screen. Nothing to hand in.

- Chapters give bigger rewards: Echoes, gear, and a full set of common gear in your first chapters.

- The live verses sit in the top right corner (tap to fold them on a phone), and the pause menu has a new Verses tab with every chapter. You can hide the list in Settings.

  • Two new pets: Waddle the Penguin and Yuzu the Capybara, for finishing chapters of First Verses.

  • A welcome for new bards. Your first run opens with one short pause: survive as long as you can, it gets harder the longer you last, and every run you come back stronger. Then small tips appear as things first happen (the horde, XP orbs, the timer, your band, Echoes, chests, gold foes), each with Next and Skip Tips. The words follow what you play with: keyboard, controller or touch.

  • Your first level-up explained. Arrows walk you through a card: what it is, what it does and its music family, what it works with, rerolls, and how to pick.

  • The Crescendo, shown. Finding the Resonant Stone pauses on a short animated lesson with your own key or button.

  • Maestra's lessons. Once you have made your first evolution, Maestra Orsolya teaches you one you have not found every few runs, so you can fill your Collection without making every evolution yourself. If your runs keep ending early, she teaches you an easy weapon to start with instead. She lets you know when you arrive in camp.

  • Save slots. A new Saves button on the title screen: three bards kept apart. Start a new bard, switch between them, or delete one (it asks first). Your name on the leaderboard is the same for all three.

Crescendos

  • No more tiers. Every Crescendo is now one Crescendo, as it is. Its colour (white, green, blue, violet, gold) only says how rare a find it is, not how strong.
  • Ten new Crescendos, each its own way to play: Echo Step (blink into the crowd and burst twice), Drumline (marching shockwaves), Mirror Ensemble (four glass bards play beside you), Black Note (pulls the crowd in, then implodes), Lullaby (puts foes to sleep: sleeping foes take double damage), Bell Tower (a great bell drops on the strongest foe), Spinning Record (a disc spirals out), Siren Song (charmed foes fight each other), Starfall (notes rain across the screen) and Pied Piper (the horde follows a lure, then it explodes).
  • Your old Crescendos carry over: owning any version of one means owning it, and the extra versions are paid back in Echoes.

Changes

  • Gentler first minutes for new bards. In your first few runs the horde eases in for longer, the tougher foes arrive a little later, and gift chests arrive at 3:00, 5:00 and 8:00.
  • No more silent surges. Before you have a companion, the boss moments used to be just a louder horde. Now they are announced ("The Silence gathers..."), and holding them off drops a chest.

Hotfix

  • The Encore is gentler. It used to get so hard so fast that three minutes of it were harder than the whole fight before it. Foes now grow tougher more slowly (about a third of the health at three minutes), and the crowd builds over six minutes instead of two. It still never ends.
  • Save slots show their bard. Each save in the Saves menu shows that bard's face, so you can tell them apart at a glance.
  • "Last played" on a save shows your own time, not UTC.
  • Save slots show their title under the bard's face.
  • Your first run's guide only shows while the game is paused. Two short pauses, one at the start and one when the first foe appears, with arrows to the real things: you, your band, health, the timer, Echoes, a foe, XP orbs, crates and pots. No more tips popping up mid-fight or over the treasure cards.
  • Power-ups and chests that drop inside walls are moved out where you can reach them (a magnet or heart from a pot in the ruins could not be picked up).
  • The Fountain only shows what you can use: Companion Might waits for your first companion, Stage Presence for the Crescendo, Echo Hunter for your first cape.
  • First Verses pays a little less early and more later: small rewards in the first chapter, growing with each chapter.
  • The death screen is easier to read: your run, what felled you, the verses you sang and the tip each have their own colour.
  • The camp's Leaderboard grows with the Sanctuary: a weathered notice board in the early camp, a timbered one later, the Hall of Legends once it is restored.
  • Fixed: after starting a new bard in Saves, the Crescendo did nothing, power-ups could not be picked up and the Journal's stats were empty. Every save now starts with all of its counters.
  • Saves never share unlocks, except the seasonal holiday vanity and Leaderboard titles, which every save has.
  • "First Verses" is now TASKS on screen and in the pause menu, with new tasks that walk you through every first system: the Armory, the Mirror, your starting weapon, Maestra, Pip, the Magic Harp, your companions and the Echo Seed. "Level a weapon to MAX" now shows your highest weapon level (x / 8).
  • Task gear arrives in a chest beside you, naming the task it is for, instead of being put on without a word.
  • The death screen has a clear order: your run in four tiles (new records marked), then damage, tasks and finds, then a tip.
  • The Ballad is softer: gentle waves with a light staff of notes, so you can see the fight through it.
  • The first card scene no longer has its tour, and the first-run guide stays clear of a phone's camera notch.
  • Task gear is spread out: the Beginner's Cape is always your first piece, task gear starts in chapter 2, and at most one task gear chest drops per run. Each is a plain chest holding just its piece, no cards.
  • The TASKS bar is smaller when folded.
  • Banish, Reroll and Skip have icons on the card screen.
  • Echo Rain is a quick grab event: a 10-second golden storm rains Echoes all around you. Grab as many as you can before it ends: the rest vanish with it.
  • Finishing a task shows "TASK COMPLETE +N Echoes" over your bard, and the Echoes fly into your run's count.
  • Tasks done in camp say "back at camp", and the first-run guide explains that runs get harder the longer they last and where to spend your Echoes.
  • Relaxed mode (Settings > Gameplay): fewer foes that hit softer and fall faster, for a calm run or for playing on the side. Relaxed runs pay 75% of their Echoes and are not ranked.
  • Weapon and boost tiles fade in fights so the screen stays clear. When your build changes, only the new tile and the ones linked to it light up for a moment. You can also keep them always shown or hide them.
  • Andy's hunts are cards now: Foe, Weapon and Land hunts each have their own coloured header and picture.
  • Loading tips rewritten: new tips for newer features and corrected old ones. Tips about things you haven't found yet stay hidden until you do.
  • On phones, the TASKS bar sits at the top right beside the counters, out of the way of the weapon tiles and pop-ups.
  • Weapon tiles now fade together with their strings.
  • Your first return to the Sanctuary is a short cutscene: it shows you the Fountain of Echoes and the gate.
  • Your first gear gets a lesson: when the Beginner's Cape comes out of your first chest, a short guide shows the six gear slots, the rarities and the cape's health gauge, with a look at the outfits you can find.
  • Breakables log: every breakable object is now listed at the end of the Journal's Foes page, with where it is found and what it can drop. The meadow's apple barrels can now be smashed.

  • Paper Crown: a new Winter Festival vanity hat in six colours (press it again in the Mirror to change colour).
  • The Mirror's bard can be turned to see hair, capes and back pieces from behind.
  • The first-run guide now mentions the weapon and boost limits and warns about enemy shots. Pop-up lines no longer slip under the timer on phones.

The Last Bard: Alpha 0.5.10 "Struck Gold"

Geysers, pools, singing crystals and falling stars full of Echoes, and a clearer War Paint.

Getting ready for the stores.

New

  • Echo Events. The golden Echo Chest is gone. Any foe may now, very rarely, set off one of four Echo Events:

- Echo Geyser: a glowing vent that erupts a column of gold.

- Echo Wellspring: a pool of liquid gold that jets up and overflows.

- Singing Geode: amber crystals that crack wider with every tier.

- Fallen Star: first a shower of shooting stars across the sky, then the star itself streaks down into a crater.

Each one stands in its own little scene, announces itself with a pop-up, and has an arrow over it and at the edge of the screen when it is out of sight. Step on it to set it off.

  • Bigger jackpots. An Echo Event climbs tier by tier, and every tier is far bigger than the last: more gold, a bigger eruption, the screen leaning in and shaking, Echoes raining from the sky near the top. Echoes clink like coins as they land, and each tier sounds bigger. Payouts now run from 15 to 400 Echoes.
  • Skipping no longer skips the fun. Pressing skip jumps straight to the final tier and still plays it.

  • The Echo Comet. Very rarely, an Echo Event is a comet: a heavier shower of gold and ice-blue stars, a frozen crater, and it always climbs to tier 3 or higher.

  • Echo Rain, a new power-up. A golden storm fills the screen for a few seconds and rains down 20 to 100 Echoes.

  • Track your Echo Events. The Journey page counts the Echo Events you have found, how many of the four kinds, your jackpots and your biggest payout. Five new achievements and three new titles to earn.
  • Don't miss one. An Echo Event about to fade warns you 15 seconds before it goes.

Changes

  • War Paint is now three simple steps: choose a paint, choose what it goes on, then confirm. Each step has big, clear choices, and the last one spells out exactly what your paint will do.

  • The card that locks in stands out. When an endless card's next pick locks it in (3/3), its ribbon turns solid gold.

  • Echo Hunter (Fountain of Echoes) now makes Echo Events more common.
  • Game stats on the Leaderboard tell the whole story:

- Weapons carried now count every weapon a run used, including ones that went into a fusion or evolution, and show how often each was fused away.

- New sections: starting weapons, each bard's averages, the average run, Crescendo scores and how grown the Fountain was.

  • Developer controls are no longer in the menus. The developer switch is gone from Settings, and the ` key no longer turns them on. They were only ever for testing, and runs played with them never counted on the leaderboard.

Fixes

  • The Mirror on iPhone no longer jumps up the page when you tap a vanity piece near the bottom.
  • World card emblems hide behind menus. They no longer cover the death screen's Echo count, the level-up cards or the pause menu.
  • Keepsake chests are safe. A chest dropping while four were already out could remove a keepsake chest you hadn't reached yet. It now only ever removes a plain chest.

Hotfix

  • War Paint never skips a step by accident. A tap on a paint or what it goes on only highlights it (in green, "Selected"); press Next to move on. The second step now says exactly what your paint does.
  • A quicker walk around the Sanctuary. Each stage of the Fountain of Echoes makes your bard walk the camp a little faster, up to a third faster at the top stage.
  • Echo Rain is bigger. It lasts longer, has its own little jingle, and some of its Echoes now land on the ground for you to pick up. A count over your bard shows how many you are getting.
  • No more stutter on your first level-up. The card screen is now prepared while the run loads, so the first one opens as smoothly as the rest (it was a big hitch on phones). A short freeze about 30 seconds into every run is gone too.
  • "Pick a card with" now covers the Endless / Loot Mode choice too, so mashing the Crescendo key when it appears never picks one by accident.
  • A new home for the game: [thelastbardgame.com](https://thelastbardgame.com). The patch notes now live there, and the game's patch notes links open it.
  • A support address: support@thelastbardgame.com, also shown on the Leaderboard page beside "Report a problem".
  • "Share anonymous run results" off now stops everything. With it off, the game no longer sends its crash and performance logs either.

The Last Bard: Alpha 0.5.9 "Clear Picks"

Cards you can read at a glance, and a pause that feels like one.

Sharper attacks, and safer card picks.

New

  • New card designs. Weapons and boosts now look different before you read a word:

- Weapons: gold on deep red, with a pointed arch around the instrument.

- Boosts: silver on midnight blue, with the picture in a laurel wreath in its music family's colour. The wreath sways gently.

- Fusions: steel blue, with the two weapons side by side under twin arches.

- Evolutions: gold and royal purple, with a rose window, a rainbow edge and both music families named.

- Companions: bronze in the companion's own colour, with their portrait in an oval frame.

Every card keeps its ornate songbook details.

  • Read a card faster.

- What the card does now comes first, in big type, with the details smaller underneath.

- Each card names its music family beside its symbol.

- The "Boosts your" / "Boosted by your" row at the bottom glows green and has room for more icons.

  • Know what works with what. Point at one of those little icons (or tap it on a phone) and it names its card. With a controller, the names show while the card is highlighted.
  • How It Works. On your very first run, a short guide explains the basics at your first level-up:

- just move and dodge;

- weapons and boosts work on their own;

- there is nothing to equip.

  • Choose which keys pick a card. Settings > Controls has three key rows:

- Crescendo key: fires the Crescendo.

- Action key: confirms in menus and picks a card. It can now be a different key from the Crescendo key.

- Pick a card with: "Enter only" or "Action key or Enter" stop a key you are spamming for the Crescendo from picking a level-up card by accident.

Number keys, clicks, taps and controllers always work.

Changes

  • The level-up screen clearly shows the game is paused. The world behind the cards turns grey and still, the music softens as if behind glass, and a line under the title says "Game paused · take your time".
  • A new weapon shows itself. When you pick a new weapon, its picture and name rise over your bard as it starts playing.
  • Twelve weapons hit the ground in style. Each one now leaves its own mark:

- Hurdy-Gurdy: toothed crank wheels grind across the ground.

- Rainstick: a downpour fills a spreading puddle.

- Gong: a bronze sunburst rolls out.

- Sitar: gourd-shaped pools ring with strings.

- War Drum: a drumhead shockwave, ropes and pegs and all.

- Soprano's Shriek: shatters like glass.

- Harp of Light: burns harp glyphs into the ground.

- Atlatl: spears land in cracked craters.

- Ballad: a ribbon of music and rose petals circles you.

- Berimbau and Kora: stamp their instruments where they land.

- Yodel: echoes out like mountain ridges.

Only the looks changed: damage and reach are the same.

  • Morwen casts three spells. An Arcane Sigil, a falling star, and a swirling Resonance Orb, in turn.
